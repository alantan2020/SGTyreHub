Car Battery Not Charging: Alternator, Belt or Battery? How to Tell

A battery that keeps going flat is either not being charged properly or cannot hold the charge it gets. The alternator, the belt that drives it, the connections and the battery itself are the four things to separate.

Car Battery Not Charging: Alternator, Belt or Battery? How to Tell

The signs

The battery warning light on the dashboard is the clearest sign that the charging system is not working. Other signs are headlights and the dashboard dimming while the engine is running, a car that starts after a jump but is flat again a short while later, and electrical items such as windows or the radio behaving slowly or oddly.

If the light comes on while you are driving, switch off any unneeded electrical items and head for a workshop. The car is running on the battery alone and will eventually stop.

What the alternator does

The battery starts the engine. Once the engine is running, the alternator takes over: it powers the car's electrical system and recharges the battery. If the alternator is failing, the battery is the only supply and is slowly emptied, however good the battery is. That is why replacing a battery without checking the charging system sometimes gives a second flat battery a few weeks later.

A simple voltage check

A multimeter set to DC volts gives a quick answer. With the engine off and the car rested for a few hours, a healthy battery usually reads around 12.4 to 12.7 volts. A reading below about 12.0 volts means it is quite flat. With the engine running, the charging system normally raises the reading to somewhere around 13.5 to 14.5 volts. If the reading with the engine running stays at the resting level, the alternator is not charging. If it is far higher than the normal range, the regulator may be faulty and overcharging the battery.

These are typical ranges, so check your handbook for your car. Take care near moving parts, and do not touch the belts or pulleys with the engine running.

Other causes to check

A slipping or broken drive belt, which you may hear as a squeal, can stop the alternator turning. Loose or corroded battery terminals, a worn earth strap and damaged wiring all reduce the charge that gets through. Our guide on terminal corrosion shows how to clean them.

On some modern cars a smart charging system deliberately holds back charge at times, so a lower reading in some conditions is not always a fault. A workshop can test it with a scan tool.

When the battery is the problem

If the charging voltage is normal and the battery is still flat, the battery is probably worn. A battery that is three to five years old, has been flat several times or is slow to crank should be tested. A workshop can check its capacity in a few minutes. Common questions

Can a bad battery damage the alternator?

A very weak battery makes the alternator work harder, which shortens its life. Fix it early.

Can I drive with the battery light on?

Only to get somewhere safe. The car is running on the battery and will stop once it is empty.

Will a jump start fix it?

It gets the engine going. If the charging fault remains, the battery will go flat again.

How long does an alternator last?

There is no fixed figure. It depends on the car and use, and many last for years before they need attention.
