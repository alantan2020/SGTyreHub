Going Overseas? Should You Disconnect Your Car Battery?

A car left parked for two or three weeks can come back with a flat battery. Disconnecting the battery is the answer people suggest most often, but on many modern cars it is not the best one. Here is how to decide.

Going Overseas? Should You Disconnect Your Car Battery?

Why a parked car drains its battery

Even when the engine is off, a modern car keeps drawing a small current. The alarm, the central locking receiver, the engine computer memory, a keyless-entry receiver and any dashcam or tracker all take a little power. On most cars this is a small trickle, but over weeks it adds up.

Singapore's heat also works against the battery. A battery that is already two or three years old has less reserve, so the same trickle that a new battery shrugs off for a month can flatten an older one in two or three weeks. There is no exact number that applies to every car, which is why the age and condition of the battery matter more than the calendar.

How long can a car sit before it is a risk

As a rough guide, a healthy battery under about three years old in a car with no extra electronics will usually cope with a trip of up to two weeks. Beyond three weeks, or with an older battery, a car that will not start on return becomes a real possibility. Cars with a dashcam in parking mode, a tracker, or a keyless system that keeps listening for the key are more likely to drain sooner.

These are general guidelines, not promises. If your battery is already slow to crank, treat it as the weak link before you leave, not after you return.

Should you disconnect the battery?

Disconnecting the negative terminal does stop the drain, and on an older, simpler car it is a reasonable choice for a long absence. On many modern cars it causes other problems. Radio presets, the clock, window and sunroof auto-close settings and the engine's learned idle values can reset. Some cars show warning lights on the next start. Many European cars, and some other makes, need a new or reconnected battery to be registered with the car's computer, and a few will not behave properly until that is done.

Also, an electronic handbrake, an electric tailgate or a keyless system can make a disconnected car awkward to move or open. Check your owner's manual first. If you are unsure, we would rather you do not disconnect it yourself, and ask for help instead.

Safer alternatives to disconnecting

Before you leave, switch off anything that is not needed: turn off dashcam parking mode, unplug phone chargers and any OBD or tracker devices that you do not need, and make sure the boot, doors and lights are fully shut. Keep the key fob well away from the car, because some keyless cars keep communicating with a fob that is close by.

If a friend or family member can start the car and drive it for at least 20 to 30 minutes once a week, that does more than idling in the carpark. A short start that does not recharge what it used can make the situation worse. A battery maintainer is the neatest option if you have a power point at your parking space, but most carparks do not.

What to do before you fly

Have the battery tested if it is more than three years old, if the engine has been slow to start, or if the car has a start-stop system. A test takes only a few minutes and tells you whether the battery is likely to survive the trip. If it is weak, it is cheaper and less stressful to replace it before you travel than to deal with a dead car at the airport carpark on your return.

If you do come back to a flat battery, do not keep trying to crank the engine. Common questions

How long can I leave my car without starting it?

A healthy battery under three years old will usually manage up to about two weeks. After three weeks, or with an older battery, a car that will not start becomes much more likely.

Is it safe to disconnect my car battery myself?

It can be fine on an older, simple car, but on many modern cars it resets settings or needs the battery to be registered with the car's computer. If you are unsure, ask first, and check your owner's manual.

Does starting the car and idling in the carpark help?

Not much. A short idle does not put back the charge the start used. A drive of 20 to 30 minutes is more useful.

Should I replace an old battery before a long trip?

If it is more than three years old or the engine has been slow to start, getting it tested before you leave is a sensible step, because replacing it on your own schedule is easier than dealing with a flat battery on return.
