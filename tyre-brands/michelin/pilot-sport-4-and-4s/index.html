Michelin Pilot Sport 4 and Pilot Sport 4 S: Two Sport Tyres, Two Different Jobs

Before the Pilot Sport 5, Michelin's performance road range was built on two tyres that look alike in a size chart and are meant for different people. This page explains the difference and what tests have found, so you can judge whether an older design is still worth buying.

The short answer

  • Pilot Sport 4: the all-round sport tyre. Michelin describes it as a high-performance summer road tyre with high reactivity on wet roads and "safety made to last". It won several magazine tests, as set out below.
  • Pilot Sport 4 S: the maximum-grip version. Michelin says it is for road and occasional track use, for drivers who "expect exceptional grip, steering precision and control, even at high speed", and notes that carmakers such as Mercedes-AMG, Ferrari and Porsche have chosen it.

If your car is a hot hatch or a quick saloon, the 4 is usually the better fit. If it is genuinely powerful, or if it sometimes goes to a track, the 4 S is the one designed for that.

How the two measured up in independent tests

These results come from Tyre Reviews and other aggregator sites that summarise Auto Bild's tests; we could not open the original German reports. They are several years old, so newer tyres may have caught up.

Auto Bild UHP 19-inch, 2020 (265/35 R19, ten tyres)Pilot Sport 4 SJoint winner with the Continental SportContact 6, and the more expensive of the two

What the pattern tells you

  1. Wet performance is the common thread. Both tyres won tests that weighted wet braking and handling, which matters in Singapore.
  2. The 4 S pays for its grip with noise and cost. In the tests above it was the loudest in one and the pricier of two winners in another.
  3. The tests are old. The Pilot Sport 5 arrived in 2022, and Michelin says it improves wet grip and tread life over the 4. If your size is available in the 5, compare it before choosing an older tyre; if it is not, the 4 is still a proven design.
