Michelin Tyres for Electric Cars: e.Primacy, Pilot Sport EV and Primacy 5 Compared

Electric cars are hard on tyres, and Singapore has plenty of them. This page explains why, and then looks at the three Michelin tyres you are most likely to be offered for an EV, including the trade-offs that the brochures leave out.

Why an EV is not just a car with a battery

Three things set EVs apart for tyres:

  1. Weight. A battery pack adds a lot of mass, which puts more load on every tyre and can wear them faster.
  2. Instant torque. An electric motor delivers its pull the moment you press the pedal, which can scrub the tread, especially on wet roads.
  3. Silence. With no engine noise to mask it, road noise from the tyres is more noticeable.

On top of those, many owners care about range, and rolling resistance is one of the few tyre properties that moves it. Michelin's Singapore advice page explains rolling resistance as the energy lost as the tyre deforms, spins through the air and rubs on the road, and adds that tyre pressure has a direct effect: it cites French road studies where under-inflation of 0.3 bar added about 6% to rolling resistance.

e.Primacy: lowest rolling resistance, with one catch

What Michelin says. The e.Primacy is "for electric, hybrid or low fuel consumption vehicles" and is chosen by carmakers as original equipment. Michelin claims rolling resistance 27% lower on average than leading competitors, credits an energy-passive compound and a slim belt that uses less material, and says electric-car drivers can expect extended range. A test site also reports a Michelin claim of up to about 7% extra range, which we could not verify independently.

What the tests found. Summaries on Tyre Reviews and similar sites report:

  • Rolling resistance: first in a 2025 AMS sustainability test (5.7 kg/t), and best in test by a clear margin at Teknikens Värld in 2025, where it placed second of eight overall in 235/55 R19.
  • Water: Tyre Reviews' summary lists curved and straight aquaplaning among its weak points, though the summaries do not say which test that comes from. We could not confirm the ranking.

The catch. Very low rolling resistance is well supported, while aquaplaning resistance is listed as a weak point in the summaries we found. In Singapore's sudden storms, that matters. Keep the pressure right, slow down in standing water and replace the tyres before the tread is low.

Pilot Sport EV: performance for powerful electric cars

What Michelin says. A high-performance summer tyre for electric vehicles, "inspired by Formula E", with 15% more cornering stiffness, a compound with a stiff centre profile to carry torque, long-lasting wet grip and reduced noise. Michelin has also published an internal comparison showing 6.7 kg/t rolling resistance against 8.8 kg/t for the Pilot Sport 4 SUV on 255/45 R19 tyres in October 2020; we could not verify it.

What the tests found. In Auto Bild's 2024 test of tyres for EVs (235/55 R19), the Pilot Sport EV was run alongside the other contenders. Tyre Reviews lists it ninth, while another report says it was left out of the final results table, so we cannot give you a reliable placing. The test was won by Michelin's own Pilot Sport 4 SUV. We could not retrieve the results of a 2023 test of EV performance tyres. The independent evidence does not yet match the marketing, so compare it with alternatives for your specific car.

Primacy 5: the comfort-first option Michelin also recommends for EVs

Michelin says the Primacy 5 is "fully suitable for EV", pointing to its tread pattern, which it designed to reduce the noise that is more noticeable in an EV, and to lower rolling resistance than its predecessor. It is not an EV-specific tyre, so ask us how it compares with the e.Primacy for your particular car. See our Primacy 5 review for the details.

Practical points for EV owners

  • Check the load index: a heavy EV needs tyres rated for its weight.
  • Rotate regularly: heavier cars with strong torque can wear the driven axle's tyres faster.
  • Check pressure monthly with the car loaded to its normal state.
