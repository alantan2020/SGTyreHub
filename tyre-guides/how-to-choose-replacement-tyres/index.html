How to Choose Replacement Tyres: Size, Rating and What Else Matters

Start with what must match

The size on your sidewall, a load index at least as high as the original and a speed rating at least as high as the original are the starting point. These come from your car, not from a brand. Our guide on reading the sidewall explains how.

Then compare what you care about

Among tyres of the right size, the differences are grip in the wet, noise, comfort, how long the tread lasts and the price. In Singapore, where heavy rain is common, wet grip is the quality most worth paying attention to.

The EU tyre label

Some tyres carry a label with ratings for wet grip, fuel efficiency and noise. Where it is present, the wet grip grade is a useful way to compare tyres on the same footing, though not every tyre sold here has the label.

Premium, mid-range and economy

Premium tyres tend to cost more and usually do better in wet grip and noise. Mid-range tyres are a balance for most people. Economy tyres cost the least and suit a car that does low mileage or short trips. The right choice depends on how much you drive, what you drive and what you value.

Replacing in sets

On a front-wheel-drive or rear-wheel-drive car, fitting a pair is common. On an all-wheel-drive car, tyres should be matched in wear, so ask before replacing only some. Always fit the better tyres at the rear if you are only replacing two.

Common questions

Is the most expensive tyre always the best choice?

Not for everyone. A premium tyre gives more margin in the wet and is quieter. If you drive little, a mid-range tyre can be a sensible choice.

Can I mix different brands on one car?

It is better to keep the same tyre on each axle. Mixing brands across axles is possible but affects handling, so ask first.

Do I need to match the original brand?

No. You need the same size and at least the same load and speed rating. The brand is a choice.
