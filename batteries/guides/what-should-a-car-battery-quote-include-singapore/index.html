What Should a Car Battery Quote Include in Singapore?

Two quotes for a car battery can differ by a lot, and the cheaper-looking one is not always cheaper once the extras are added. Here is what to check so you compare like with like.

What Should a Car Battery Quote Include in Singapore?

Start with the exact battery, not just the car

A quote should name the brand, the model line and the battery code, for example 55B24L or LN3, not just 'battery for your car'. The same car can take more than one battery depending on the year, the engine and whether it has a start-stop system. If a quote does not say which battery it is, you cannot tell what you are being offered. Our guide on battery codes explains how to read the label on your current battery.

Standard, EFB or AGM: is it the right type for your car?

A car with a start-stop system normally needs an EFB or AGM battery. Fitting a cheaper standard battery in place of one can shorten its life. If the quote is lower because the type is lower, that is not a like-for-like saving. Our guide on EFB, AGM and standard batteries explains the difference.

Is fitting included, and where is it done?

Some quotes show the battery alone and add fitting later. Ask whether the price covers removing the old battery, installing the new one and checking that the car starts and charges. Also ask whether the work is done at a workshop or where your car is parked, and whether that changes the price.

What happens to the old battery?

Used car batteries must be disposed of properly, so a fair quote says whether the old battery is taken away and whether that is already in the price. Some sellers give a trade-in credit for the old battery. Ask whether the figure you were given is before or after any such credit.

European cars: registration or coding

Many European cars need the new battery to be registered with the car's electronics after a replacement. If your car is one of them, ask whether registration or coding is included, who does it, and what it costs if it is extra. Our guide on European car battery registration explains why this step matters.

GST, warranty and what is extra

Ask whether the price already includes GST, because a quote before GST and a quote after GST are not comparable. Ask how long the warranty is, what it covers and what it does not, and what you need to show to make a claim. Our guide on battery warranty questions lists what to ask. Keep the terms in writing, and be careful with any promise that sounds too good to be true.

How to get a quote you can compare

Take a clear photo of the label on your current battery and note your car's make, model and year. With those details a seller can tell you the exact battery, what is included and what is extra. Send the photo to us on WhatsApp and we will confirm the right battery and quote you.

Common questions

Why do two quotes for the same car differ so much?

They are often not for the same thing. The battery brand, the battery type (standard, EFB or AGM), whether fitting and old battery disposal are included, and the warranty can all differ. Compare the battery code and the list of what is included before comparing the price.

Should the old battery be taken away in the quoted price?

A fair quote says what happens to the old battery. Ask whether disposal is included and whether any trade-in credit has already been taken off the price.

Do I need to register the new battery on my car?

Many European cars do, and some others with a battery management system may as well. Check your car's requirements and ask whether registration or coding is included in the quote.
