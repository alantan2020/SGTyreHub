How to read your car battery label

One clear photo of the label is the fastest way to get the right replacement battery.

Where to find it

Open the bonnet and look for the battery, a box with two terminals (+ and −). The label is usually on top or on the side facing you. In some cars the battery is under a seat or in the boot, and a plastic cover may hide it. If you cannot reach it safely, send us your car model and year instead and we will tell you what to check.

What to photograph

  • The whole label, in good light, with the code readable.
  • The side with the two terminals, so we can see which side the positive (+) terminal is on.
  • Any words such as EFB, AGM or Start-Stop.

What the code looks like

Japanese-size batteries carry a code such as 55D23L or 95D26L. The last letter, L or R, tells which side the positive terminal is on, so a wrong letter will not fit the cables. European-size batteries carry a code such as DIN66 or LN3. If you are not sure, just send the photo, you do not need to read it.

Stop-start, EFB and AGM

If your car switches its engine off at traffic lights, or the label says EFB or AGM, the new battery must be the same type or better. Please do not replace it with a standard battery.
