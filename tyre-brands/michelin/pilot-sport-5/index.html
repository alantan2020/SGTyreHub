Michelin Pilot Sport 5: Six Questions Drivers Ask Before Buying

The Pilot Sport 5 is Michelin's current sport tyre for the road. Michelin introduced it in January 2022 together with the Primacy 4+, and called it the successor to the Pilot Sport 4. Below are the six questions we hear most often, answered with what Michelin says and what other people's tests found.

1. Is it a tyre for my car?

Michelin's answer is "drivers who want precise steering and grip, and a tyre that lasts longer than older sport tyres". In its driving-style guide it hands the Pilot Sport 5 to the driver who is "always ready, usually packed", someone who takes spontaneous road trips and wants confidence on wet motorways.

In practice that means a sporty or premium car, or any car whose owner enjoys driving. A family MPV used for school runs does not need it; the Primacy 5 is the tyre Michelin recommends for that life. Press reports at launch said it came in about 50 sizes from 17 to 21 inches, so it is also aimed at cars with larger wheels.

2. What actually changed from the Pilot Sport 4?

Michelin lists these, in its own words, as the main differences: better grip on wet roads, a tougher tread that lasts longer, a more refined feel, a premium-looking sidewall finish and a wider range of sizes. Three named technologies carry the story:

  • MaxTouch Construction. Michelin says it spreads the forces of accelerating, braking and cornering more evenly across the part of the tyre that touches the road, which should slow wear.
  • Dynamic Response Technology. The tyre structure is designed so that steering input reaches the road quickly, which is where the "precise" feeling comes from.
  • Dual Sport Tread Design. One half of the tread has wide grooves for pushing water away, the other has stiffer blocks for dry grip.

Michelin also states a typical speed rating of Y, which means over 300 km/h under controlled conditions. You will not use that on a Singapore road, but it tells you the tyre is built to a high standard of heat and stress tolerance.

3. Is it good in the rain?

Michelin describes the Pilot Sport 5 as a "100% road tyre" whose first priority is wet performance, so that the driver can push hard "in full control". We looked for independent test placings to set against that. The summaries we could read were inconsistent about which tyres won the 2022 and 2023 tests that included the Pilot Sport 5, and we could not open the original reports, so we are not publishing placings we cannot confirm. What we can say is that aggregator listings generally show it among the better wet performers rather than at the bottom, and that its predecessor, the Pilot Sport 4, has a well-documented record of test wins, which we set out on our Pilot Sport 4 and 4 S page. If wet braking is your deciding factor, ask us for the latest independent tests in your size and we will tell you what they show.

4. How is it different from the Pilot Sport 4 S?

Michelin says the two are "complementary". The Pilot Sport 5 is a pure road tyre, designed for wet performance, steering precision on dry roads and longevity. The Pilot Sport 4 S is aimed at the most powerful cars and, in Michelin's words, is about 80% road and 20% track, built for maximum dry grip. If you do not drive on a circuit, Michelin's own comparison points you to the 5. We cover the 4 and 4 S on their own page.

5. Is it quiet and comfortable?

Michelin does not make a silence claim for it on its Singapore page. Sport tyres generally trade some comfort for steering feel; in an older test the Pilot Sport 4 S, a sibling, was marked down for noise. If a quiet cabin is your priority, choose the Primacy 5.

6. Will it fit, and how do I know?

Not every size exists, and some manufacturers approve particular tyres. Read the size on your sidewall (for example 225/45 R17 94W), check the load and speed ratings against your car's requirements, and send us the photo. We will tell you whether the Pilot Sport 5 exists in your size and what the alternatives are.
