Run-Flat Tyres in Singapore: How Far You Can Drive, Repair Limits and What to Know

A run-flat tyre is built to carry the car for a limited distance after losing air, so you can reach a safe place or a workshop instead of changing a wheel at the roadside. It is a useful feature, but with limits that many drivers do not know.

Run-Flat Tyres in Singapore: How Far You Can Drive, Repair Limits and What to Know

How a run-flat works

A normal tyre collapses when the air is lost, and the sidewall folds under the car's weight. A run-flat has a reinforced sidewall that can carry the load for a while with no pressure. Some designs use a stiffened sidewall, others use an inner support ring. Either way, the tyre keeps its shape long enough for you to get to a safe stop.

Because it can keep going quietly, you may not feel a puncture. That is why cars fitted with run-flats are normally fitted with a tyre pressure monitoring system that warns you on the dashboard.

How far and how fast

The usual figure quoted by tyre makers is a limited distance, often around 80 km, at a reduced speed, often no more than 80 km/h. The exact figures depend on the tyre, the load and how much air is left, so use the numbers in your car's handbook and on the tyre maker's information, not a general rule.

Drive gently, avoid hard braking and turning, and head for the nearest safe place. Do not treat the distance as a way to finish a long journey.

Repair: usually not

A run-flat that has been driven with no pressure can be damaged inside even when the outside looks fine, and many tyre makers advise that such a tyre is not repaired and should be replaced. Some makers allow a repair in limited cases when the tyre has not been driven flat. This varies, so follow the guidance for your tyre and let the workshop inspect the inside before deciding.

In practice, if you have had to drive on a flat run-flat, expect to replace the tyre.

Costs and ride

Run-flats are usually more expensive than ordinary tyres of the same size, and the stiffer sidewall can make the ride firmer and a little noisier on rough roads. In return you carry less, since cars designed for run-flats often have no spare wheel at all. Some makers sell run-flats and standard tyres in the same model line, so check which one your car needs.

Can I swap to normal tyres?

It is possible on many cars, but it should be done thoughtfully. Without a spare wheel and with a car set up for run-flats, a swap leaves you with nothing to use after a puncture, and the ride and handling will change. Do not mix run-flat and normal tyres on the same axle. Ask before you swap, and check your tyre pressure warning still works correctly.

Common questions

How do I know if my tyres are run-flats?

Look on the sidewall for markings such as RFT, ROF, ZP, SSR or the words Run Flat, depending on the maker. Your car handbook also says.

Do I need a spare if I have run-flats?

Many cars with run-flats come without a spare wheel. If yours has none, a flat that cannot be driven on needs a tow or a mobile fix.

Can I put run-flats on a car that came with normal tyres?

In general you can, but check the load rating and your car's pressure monitoring. Ask before you buy.

Do run-flats wear out faster?

Not necessarily. Wear depends on the tyre, your driving and your alignment. Check pressures and rotate as usual.
