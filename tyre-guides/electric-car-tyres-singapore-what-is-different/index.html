Electric Car Tyres in Singapore: What Is Different and What to Look For

An electric car is not much different to drive, but it is hard on tyres. More weight and instant torque change how tyres wear, and the right replacement depends on more than the size.

Electric Car Tyres in Singapore: What Is Different and What to Look For

Why EVs are harder on tyres

Electric cars carry a heavy battery, often several hundred kilograms more than a similar petrol car, and deliver their power instantly. Both effects load the tyres, especially the driven wheels. Many owners find that tyres wear faster than on their previous car, particularly if they use the acceleration or drive mainly in stop-start city traffic.

That makes the basics matter more: correct pressure, rotation on schedule and alignment checks.

What tyre makers change for EVs

Tyres sold as EV tyres are normally built to deal with the extra weight, to keep rolling resistance low so that the battery goes further, and to keep road noise down, since there is no engine noise to hide it. Some use a stronger construction or a higher load rating, and some carry foam inside the tyre to reduce cabin noise.

These are the makers' design goals. How much difference they make depends on the tyre and the car, so read the specification and not just the badge.

Markings to look for

Check the load index on your current tyres. An EV needs a tyre rated for the car's weight, and that is shown on the sidewall and in the handbook. Some tyres carry an XL or reinforced marking.

Some car makers also approve particular tyres for their cars, and mark them with a code on the sidewall. If your car came with a marked tyre, ask whether a replacement without the mark is suitable, and what that means for noise and range.

Rotation, pressure and alignment

Rotate the tyres at the interval in your handbook, often earlier than on a light petrol car. Check the pressure monthly: EVs are sensitive to low pressure, which raises rolling resistance and cuts range. Uneven wear is a sign to check alignment. Our guides on rotation and balancing and on tyre pressure explain how.

Choosing a replacement

Start with the size and load index on your current tyres. Then decide what matters most: comfort and quietness, range, or grip. Do not choose by a single label. Common questions

Do I need special tyres for an EV?

Not always, but the tyre must carry the car's weight and suit its needs. EV-specific tyres are designed for that.

Will EV tyres improve range?

Low rolling resistance can help, but the effect depends on the tyre and how you drive. Pressure matters at least as much.

How often should I rotate EV tyres?

Follow the handbook. Many owners check every 8,000 to 10,000 km.

Are EV tyres noisier or quieter?

Many are designed to be quieter, since there is no engine noise to mask them. Compare the maker's information for the models you are choosing between.
