BYD Sealion 7 Premium vs Tesla Model Y: Weekdays in Singapore, Weekend Charging in Johor

Picture a simple week: short drives in Singapore from Monday to Friday, then a weekend at a home in Johor where you can install a charger. Two popular electric SUVs fit that routine, the BYD Sealion 7 Premium and the Tesla Model Y. Here is how they compare, using published prices, specifications and reviews. Prices and COE move every two weeks, so treat the numbers as a snapshot from November 2026.

BYD Sealion 7 Premium vs Tesla Model Y: Weekdays in Singapore, Weekend Charging in Johor

The short answer

For a routine of about 200 km a week with a weekend charge at home, both cars have more range than you need. That makes price, ride, noise and everyday features matter more than the biggest range figure.

The Sealion 7 Premium costs less and supports Apple CarPlay and Android Auto. The Model Y rides more tightly, is quicker, and has Tesla's own charging and service network around Johor. It is also the noisier of the two on the road, according to several reviews.

Price and range side by side

BYD Singapore advertises the Sealion 7 Premium with COE, and a Motorist review lists it in VES Band A1. Its battery is 82.5 kWh with a 480 km WLTP range, and BYD quotes a 10-year or 200,000 km battery warranty.

Tesla's Singapore line-up in an August 2026 dealer price list ran from the Premium RWD (62.5 kWh) to the Long Range RWD (84 kWh, up to 661 km WLTP) and the six-seat Model Y L (88 kWh, 681 km). In the listings we compared, the Model Y Long Range was roughly 12 to 22 percent more expensive than the Sealion 7 Premium, for about 180 km more rated range, and the Model Y L was roughly a quarter to a third more.

  • Sealion 7 Premium: 82.5 kWh, 480 km WLTP, about 230 kW, 0 to 100 km/h in about 6.7 seconds.
  • Model Y Long Range RWD: 84 kWh, up to 661 km WLTP.
  • UK road tests put the Model Y RWD at about 5.9 seconds to 100 km/h, against 6.7 for the Sealion 7.

How they drive

Reviews describe the Model Y as firm but composed, with less body lean and a more agile feel. The Sealion 7 is softer; one review called its ride wallowy as well as firm, and reviewers noticed the body leaning in corners.

Road noise is the most repeated complaint about the Model Y, including in Singapore reviews, with one noting noise from the tyres at around 80 km/h. Later cars with revised suspension are reported to be quieter. We did not find a comparable noise comment for the Sealion 7, so that is not a verdict either way.

The Model Y's regenerative braking cannot be switched off completely. It has a hold mode for one-pedal driving, but small speed adjustments by lifting off the accelerator take some getting used to.

Everyday features

The Sealion 7 supports Apple CarPlay and Android Auto. The Model Y does not support either, so navigation and music run through Tesla's own system.

BYD lists a nine-airbag safety package and a 360-degree camera for the Sealion 7, and 6/4 split-fold rear seats with up to 1,789 litres of cargo space. The Sealion 7 measures 4,830 mm long and 1,925 mm wide. We did not find comparable Model Y dimensions and boot figures from a source we trust, so check those on a test drive.

Charging between Johor and Singapore

At a landed home in Johor, charging is the same for both cars: a wallbox on a dedicated circuit. Published guides put a 7 kW single-phase installation at roughly RM3,500 to RM8,000, and a TNB supply upgrade at RM200 to RM3,500 if the existing supply is too small. Domestic TNB electricity is about 27 to 28 sen per kWh, and a time-of-use plan has off-peak rates of about 24 sen.

As an illustration, a car that uses 18 kWh per 100 km needs about 36 kWh for 200 km, which a 7 kW charger delivers in about five hours. Your own car's efficiency will differ.

Tesla owners also have Superchargers in Johor, including Sunway Big Box in Iskandar Puteri, and Tesla has announced shared service coverage across Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand. We did not find a source confirming whether other brands can use Tesla Superchargers in Malaysia, so check before you rely on it.

Which one suits which driver

Choose by how you actually drive.

  • Pick the Sealion 7 Premium if you want a lower price, CarPlay or Android Auto, and a softer ride for mostly short trips.
  • Pick the Model Y if you value a tighter, faster feel, expect longer trips where Tesla's chargers help, and can live with more road noise.
  • Test drive both on a road you know. Ride and noise are personal.

Common questions

Is the Sealion 7 Premium cheaper than the Model Y in Singapore?

In the listings we compared, yes: the Sealion 7 Premium was below every Model Y variant we found except possibly the entry Premium RWD, where the gap was small. COE changes every two weeks, so ask for a current quote with COE.

Which has the longer range?

The Model Y Long Range RWD is rated up to 661 km WLTP against 480 km for the Sealion 7 Premium. For about 200 km a week, both are more than enough.

Does the Model Y support Apple CarPlay?

No. The Sealion 7 supports Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, while the Model Y does not support either.

Can I charge at home in Johor with a Singapore-registered car?

We did not find a rule that stops it. Charging at your own home uses a home charger on your own supply, and the installer will check the supply and the approvals.
