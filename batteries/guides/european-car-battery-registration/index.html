Why European Cars May Need Battery Registration (Coding)

On many European cars, swapping the battery is not just a spanner job. The car's computer wants to be told that a new battery has been fitted.

Why European Cars May Need Battery Registration (Coding)

What battery registration is

Many European cars, including many BMW, Mercedes-Benz, Audi, Volkswagen, Volvo, MINI and Land Rover models, manage the charging of the battery with a computer. The computer tracks the battery's age, type and capacity, and it adjusts how hard the alternator charges it. When a new battery is fitted, the computer must be told, using a diagnostic tool. This is often called registering or coding the battery.

What can happen if you skip it

If the new battery is not registered, the car may keep charging it as if it were old and worn. That can shorten the new battery's life. Some cars also show warnings, switch off the stop-start system, or limit comfort features to save power.

The battery is often AGM or EFB, and in an unusual place

Many European cars use AGM or EFB batteries. The battery may be in the boot, under a seat or under the floor. The new battery must be the same type or better and the right size, including its venting arrangement, which is why a standard battery is not a safe substitute. See our guide to EFB and AGM batteries.

Not every European car is the same

The need for registration depends on the make, the model and the year. Some older European cars do not need it. Newer ones usually do. This is why a general answer is not enough, and why we ask for your car model and year, as well as a photo of the battery label.

How we handle European cars

We do not quote a fixed price for European cars straight away. We collect your car model, year, location and a photo of the battery label, and the person in charge confirms the right battery and how the replacement will be done. The reply is from a person, not instant.

Common questions

Can I change the battery in my BMW or Mercedes myself?

It is physically possible on some models, but a new battery usually needs to be registered with the car's computer. Without that, the battery may wear out early.

Does registration apply to Japanese cars too?

Most Japanese cars do not need it. Some newer models with advanced charging control may. Send us your car model and year.

What about electric cars and hybrids?

They have a separate 12V battery that powers the car's systems. It is often a special type, and the procedure can be different. We confirm each case with the person in charge.
