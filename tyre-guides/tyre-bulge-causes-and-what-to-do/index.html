Tyre Bulge: Causes, Is It Safe to Drive and What to Do in Singapore

A bulge in the side of a tyre is not a cosmetic problem. It means the structure inside the tyre has been damaged, and the tyre has to be replaced. Here is what has happened inside it and what to do if you find one.

Tyre Bulge: Causes, Is It Safe to Drive and What to Do in Singapore

What a bulge actually is

A tyre is not just rubber. Under the rubber is a layer of fabric and steel cords that hold the shape of the tyre and contain the air pressure. A bulge, sometimes called a bubble or a blister, appears when some of those cords have snapped or separated. The air inside then pushes the weakened rubber outward, like a small balloon on the side of the tyre.

Because the cords are what carry the load, a bulge is a sign that the tyre can no longer hold its shape reliably at speed. It is more likely to burst, and it can do so without warning.

What causes it

By far the most common cause is a sharp impact. Hitting a pothole, an open drain cover, a raised manhole or the edge of a kerb pinches the sidewall between the road and the wheel rim and breaks the cords inside, even when the outside rubber looks unmarked. The bulge may not show up straight away; it can appear hours or days later.

Other causes are driving with very low pressure, which makes the sidewall flex and overheat, overloading the car, and a manufacturing or age-related fault, which is much rarer. In Singapore, tight multi-storey car parks and kerbs at pick-up points are a common source of sidewall knocks.

If you find a bulge: what to do

Do not top up the air to see if it goes down, and do not carry on with a long journey. Slow down, drive gently to a safe place and do not use expressways on a tyre you suspect.

Put the spare on if you have one, or call your roadside assistance, your insurer's helpline or a workshop. If the bulge is on a tyre that you cannot change at the roadside, drive only the short distance to a safe stop at low speed. Then have the tyre looked at before using the car normally.

Can a bulge be repaired?

No. A bulge is structural damage, and there is no safe way to repair a damaged sidewall. Patches and plugs are only used for small punctures in the central tread, not for the sidewall. The tyre has to be replaced.

Check the other tyres at the same time, especially the one on the other side of the same axle. Whatever hit one wheel often hit the other, and a second tyre may have hidden damage. It is also worth having the wheel itself checked for a bend.

Will a warranty cover it?

Damage from hitting a pothole or a kerb is normally treated as road damage, and most tyre makers' warranties exclude it. Some retailers or insurers offer separate road-hazard cover. Check the terms of whatever you bought, and keep a photo of the bulge and the tyre's DOT code.

Common questions

Is it safe to drive with a small bulge?

No. A small bulge can be a sign of a lot of broken cords, and the size does not tell you how close the tyre is to failing. Replace it.

Can a bulge appear on a new tyre?

Yes, if it hits a pothole or kerb hard enough. A very new tyre with a bulge and no impact is rare, but worth showing to the supplier straight away.

Is a bulge on the tread the same as on the sidewall?

Both are structural damage. A bulge on the tread surface can come from a separated belt. In either case the tyre should be taken off.

Do I replace one tyre or two?

Usually one, if the others are in good condition and not far worn. If the rest of the set is much more worn, a pair on the same axle may suit the car better. Send us your car model and tyre size and we will advise.
