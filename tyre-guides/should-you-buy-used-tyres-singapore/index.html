Should You Buy Used or Second-Hand Tyres in Singapore?

A used tyre can look fine and still be a poor choice. The problem is not what you can see, it is what you cannot: how old it is, how it was driven, and whether it was damaged or repaired before. If you are still thinking about used tyres, these are the checks that matter.

By SG Tyre Hub · Published 26 November 2026

Should You Buy Used or Second-Hand Tyres in Singapore?

What you cannot see

Under the tread, a tyre is made of fabric and steel cords. A hard knock against a kerb or a pothole can break those cords without leaving a mark on the outside, and a bulge can appear later. A used tyre has no record of what it went through, which is the main reason to be careful.

Check the date first

Look for the DOT code on the sidewall. The last four digits show the week and year of manufacture. An older used tyre may already be near the end of its useful life even if the tread looks deep. Our guide on the DOT date code shows how to read it.

Look for bulges, cracks and damage

Check both sidewalls and the inner side as well. A bulge, a bubble, deep cracks or cuts mean the tyre should not be used. Our guide on tyre bulges explains why a bulge cannot be repaired and the tyre has to be replaced.

Ask about previous repairs

Only a small, clean puncture in the central tread is generally considered repairable, and it must be repaired properly from the inside. A used tyre with an unknown repair history, or with several repairs close together, is a risk. Our guide on whether a punctured tyre can be repaired explains the limits.

Check the tread, and buy as a matching set

The legal minimum tread depth for cars in Singapore is 1.6 mm, so a used tyre with little tread left gives you little life. If you buy used, tyres on the same axle should match in size and wear, because uneven tyres affect handling.

When used tyres are not the right choice

If the car is used for long expressway drives, carries heavy loads, or you cannot confirm the age and condition of the tyre, a used tyre is not worth the risk. Safety is the one place where it does not pay to guess.

Common questions

Is it safe to buy used tyres?

It depends on the individual tyre. You cannot see internal damage from the outside, so check the date, look for bulges and cracks, and ask about any repairs. If you cannot confirm these, the risk is higher.

Can a bulge in a used tyre be repaired?

No. A bulge means the cords inside the tyre have been damaged, and the tyre has to be replaced.

How do I find out how old a tyre is?

Read the DOT code on the sidewall. The last four digits are the week and year it was made.
