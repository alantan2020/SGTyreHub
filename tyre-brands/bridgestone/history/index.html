Bridgestone Tyres: History from Kurume to Formula 1

Bridgestone began as the tyre division of a company that made traditional Japanese footwear. This is its story as Bridgestone itself tells it.

The company facts on this page come from Bridgestone's own corporate history and profile pages, which we read on 18 November 2026. Where a date or fact is not on those pages, we have left it out rather than guess.

1. From a tabi-sock maker to a tyre company

Bridgestone did not start as a tyre company. Its predecessor was a division of Nihon Tabi Company, a maker of tabi, the traditional Japanese split-toe socks and footwear. In 1930 that division began producing the first tyres. A year later, on 1 March 1931, Shojiro Ishibashi established Bridgestone Tire Co., Ltd in Kurume, in Fukuoka Prefecture.

The company changed its name twice before settling. In 1942 it became Nippon Tire Co., Ltd, in 1951 it went back to Bridgestone Tire Co., Ltd, and in 1984 it became Bridgestone Corporation, the name it still uses. Ishibashi died in 1976. He was inducted into the Automotive Hall of Fame in 2006.

2. Radial tyres and going global

Radial construction, where the cords run across the tyre rather than diagonally, is what almost every modern car tyre uses. Bridgestone's history page records two firsts for Japan: in 1962 it made Japan's first steel radial tyres, the Steel Rib, for trucks and buses, and in 1964 it made Japan's first radial tyres for passenger cars.

Its biggest international step came in the 1980s. In 1983 it bought Firestone's plant in Nashville, Tennessee, its first production plant in the United States. In 1988 it merged with the Firestone Tire & Rubber Company, then the second-largest tyre maker in the United States, for 2.6 billion US dollars.

3. Motorsport

Bridgestone entered Formula 1 in 1997. The company's history page notes that in 1998 a McLaren car on Bridgestone tyres, driven by Mika Häkkinen, won the Japanese Grand Prix. In 1995, Firestone, by then part of the group, returned to Indy car racing and the Indianapolis 500 after twenty years away.
