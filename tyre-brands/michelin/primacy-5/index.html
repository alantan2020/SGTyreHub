Common questions

Is the Primacy 5 quiet on a Singapore expressway?

The difference Michelin reports is small: 1.64 dB in a test it commissioned on a large SUV with 265/60 R18 tyres. It may differ on your car and size, and road surface matters too, so do not expect silence.

Is it good in heavy rain?

It is a sound wet tyre and Michelin's figures are strong, but it did not lead every braking test. Keep the pressure right and replace it before it is worn out.

Does it suit an electric car?

Michelin says yes. For maximum range, the e.Primacy is Michelin's dedicated option.

Full review, test details and sources

Verdict in one minute. The Primacy 5 is the Michelin to look at if your week is made of school runs, office commutes and the occasional drive up to Malaysia. Michelin sells it on comfort, quietness, mileage and braking in the wet. Independent tests back the comfort, wear and efficiency story, but they do not show it winning on grip, so it is a "sensible and refined" tyre, not a "fastest around the corner" one. If you want sharp steering, look at the Pilot Sport 5. If your car is an electric one and range is your first worry, read our guide to Michelin's EV tyres.

What the Primacy 5 is for

Michelin's own wording is "a confident choice for everyday driving with a premium comfort ride and excellent longevity". In its "which tyre suits your driving style" guide it gives this tyre to drivers who treat the car as a calm place to be: quiet cabin, reliable braking in the rain, and a tyre that is not worn out by the next service.

That is a useful clue about who should buy it. If you recognise yourself in the description, you will probably be happy. If you read it and think "that sounds dull", you are a Pilot Sport driver and you should skip this page.

Three claims, and what stands behind each

1. "Quiet and comfortable"

Michelin says the Primacy 5 is ranked first for silence and comfort and credits a new tread pattern and a vibration-damping technology it calls Silent Rib Gen 3. The evidence Michelin shows is from tests it ordered: TÜV Rheinland Thailand measured a Honda Accord e:HEV fitted with 225/50 R17 tyres in November 2024, and later a large SUV on 265/60 R18 in November 2025, where Michelin reports 3% (1.64 dB) lower interior noise than other premium brands. A difference of 1.64 dB is small. Real-world quietness also depends on your road surface and your car's insulation, so treat "quiet" as "among the quieter tyres", not as a promise of silence.

2. "Safe in the wet, new or worn"

This is the claim to weigh most carefully, because it matters in Singapore's downpours. Michelin reports shorter braking from 80 to 0 km/h than the average of other premium brands: 8% shorter new and 13% shorter worn on the Accord, 16% shorter in both states on the SUV. Those are Michelin-ordered tests. Other comparisons are less one-sided. In a 2024 test of five premium touring tyres in 205/55 R16 on a Golf, run by the labs TÜV SÜD and DEKRA and reported by Tyre Reviews, the Continental PremiumContact 7 had the shortest wet and dry braking, while the Primacy 5 was best on wear and efficiency. That test was commissioned by Michelin, so it is not an independent cross-check of Michelin's own figures, but it does show where a rival came out ahead. A fair reading is that its wet behaviour is good, that it is not the braking leader in every test, and that its older sibling, the Primacy 4, was the one Michelin designed around wet braking above all else.

3. "Lasts longer"

Michelin says the Primacy 5 gives 18% more mileage than the Primacy 4+, measured on an electric vehicle, and that DEKRA measured 24% longer life than other premium brands on a Golf VIII with 205/55 R16 tyres. The same Michelin-commissioned test agrees that wear and efficiency are its strengths, and Tyre Reviews' own weighting of it (not a result issued by the labs) put the Primacy 5 and the PremiumContact 7 in joint first place, the Michelin ahead on abrasion and rolling resistance and the Continental ahead on braking. Tyre Reviews' statistics page also shows an average finish of about fourth across the tests it tracks, with a best of first and a worst of seventh. Mileage depends heavily on your driving, your alignment and your tyre pressure, so use these numbers to compare tyres with each other, not to predict your own kilometres.

A note on the Energy XM2+

The Energy XM2+ is an older long-life design that Michelin sells for "city commutes and weekend drives". Michelin says it gives about 25% more mileage than the average of premium competitors; that figure comes from a CATARC test Michelin ordered in September 2018, run over 20,000 km on a VW Golf 7 with 205/55 R16 tyres. We found no fully independent long-term wear test. Reports of its launch date point to January 2020 as the successor to the Energy XM2. It does not claim the Primacy 5's quietness, so if you drive a lot and price per kilometre matters more than refinement, ask us whether your size exists in the XM2+ as well as the Primacy 5.

Where the Primacy 5 came from

Michelin unveiled the Primacy 4+ in January 2022, alongside the Pilot Sport 5, and the Primacy 5 is its successor. We could not find an official launch date for the Primacy 5 itself. Michelin describes the shift between generations like this: the Primacy 4 emphasised wet braking, the Primacy 5 emphasises comfort and efficiency while still aiming at strong wet braking from the first kilometre to the last.

Before you buy

  1. Photograph your current tyre's sidewall: the size, load index and speed rating must match or exceed what your car needs.
  2. Read the four-digit date code on any tyre you are offered, so you know how old it is.
  3. If you tow, carry heavy loads or have a long KL drive planned, tell us; load rating and pressure matter more than the brand name.

Questions people ask about the Primacy 5

Is it really quiet on a Singapore expressway? Michelin's tests show a small noise advantage; road surface matters as much. If noise is your main worry, ask about your specific car.

Does it suit an electric car? Michelin says yes, and it highlights the noise-reducing tread as more noticeable in an EV. For maximum range, the e.Primacy is Michelin's dedicated option.

Is it good in heavy rain? It is a sound wet tyre, and Michelin's figures are strong, but independent results do not put it at the very top. Keep the pressure right and replace it before it is worn out.

Primacy 5 or Pilot Sport 5? Comfort and mileage against steering feel and grip. Choose the one that matches how you drive on a normal Tuesday.
