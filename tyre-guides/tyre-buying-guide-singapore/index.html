Buying Tyres in Singapore: Size, Date, Quote and What to Check

Buying tyres is easier when you know what to check. This checklist covers the size, the age of the tyre, what a quote should include and the questions worth asking, using plain steps you can follow without any special tools.

Buying Tyres in Singapore: Size, Date, Quote and What to Check

Step 1: Find your tyre size

The size is printed on the sidewall of the tyre you have now, for example 205/55R16 91V. It gives the width, the height of the sidewall, the rim size, the load rating and the speed rating. Our guide on how to read your tyre sidewall explains each part. If you change the size, check that it still suits your car, because the wrong size can affect the speedometer, the clearance and the handling.

Step 2: Check the date on the tyre

Every tyre has a DOT code, and the last four digits show the week and year it was made. For example, 2623 means the 26th week of 2023. A tyre that has sat in storage for a long time can be past its best even if it looks new, so ask for the date before you buy. Our guide on the DOT date code shows where to look.

Step 3: Know why you are replacing them

The legal minimum tread depth for cars in Singapore is 1.6 mm, but tread is not the only reason to replace a tyre. Cracks in the sidewall, a bulge, or a vibration that was not there before are also reasons to have the tyre checked. Our guide on when to replace car tyres covers the signs.

Step 4: Choose what suits how you drive

Beyond the size, think about how you use the car: mostly short city trips, long expressway drives, wet roads or a heavy load. Our guide on how to choose replacement tyres explains size, rating and the other things that matter, so you can describe what you need rather than only asking for a brand.

Step 5: Compare quotes like for like

A tyre quote should state the brand, the model and the size, and whether fitting, balancing, valve and disposal of the old tyres are included. It should also say whether GST is included. Two quotes can look very different and still not be comparable. Our guide on what a tyre price should include gives a checklist.

Step 6: Set the pressure after fitting

Once new tyres are on, check the pressure against the sticker in the driver's door frame or the fuel flap, not the number on the tyre sidewall, which is the maximum. Check when the tyres are cold, once a month and before a long drive. Our guide on tyre pressure explains why.

Common questions

Where do I find my tyre size?

On the sidewall of the tyre you have now. It looks like 205/55R16 91V. The first three numbers give the width, the sidewall height and the rim size.

How old is too old for a new tyre?

The date is shown in the last four digits of the DOT code. Ask for it before buying, because a tyre stored for a long time can be past its best even if it has never been used.

What should I check on a tyre quote?

The brand, model and size, whether fitting, balancing and disposal of old tyres are included, and whether GST is included.
