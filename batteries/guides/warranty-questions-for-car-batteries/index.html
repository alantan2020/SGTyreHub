Car Battery Warranty: What to Ask Before You Buy

A warranty is only useful if you know what it covers and how to claim. These are the questions worth asking before you pay for any car battery.

Car Battery Warranty: What to Ask Before You Buy

What a battery warranty usually covers

A battery warranty generally covers manufacturing defects, such as an internal fault that makes the battery fail early under normal use. It usually does not cover damage from misuse or from a fault elsewhere in the car.

What it usually does not cover

Typical exclusions are a cracked or damaged case, a battery that has been deeply drained repeatedly, a faulty alternator or electrical fault that damages the battery, incorrect installation, and wear from normal ageing. A battery of the wrong type for the car may also be excluded.

Questions to ask the seller

Ask these before you agree to buy, and ask for the answers in writing in the chat or on the receipt:

  • How long is the warranty, and does it start from the day of replacement?
  • Is it a free replacement for the whole period, or does the value reduce over time?
  • What do I need to bring to make a claim? (Usually the receipt and the battery.)
  • Who will test the battery, and how long does the process take?
  • Is the warranty the same for standard, EFB and AGM batteries? Warranty periods often differ by type.

Keep your proof

Keep the receipt or the WhatsApp conversation, and note the date of replacement. Do not remove or damage the battery label, because the claim is checked against the code on it.

The old battery

Ask what happens to your old battery. In a proper replacement, the old battery is taken away for recycling, and you should not leave it on the road or in a bin. Car batteries contain lead and acid and must be recycled.

Common questions

Does the warranty start when I buy or when I fit?

Ask the seller. For a replacement done at the same time, they are usually the same day. Always keep the receipt or chat as proof of the date.

Is a longer warranty always better?

Not necessarily. Check what is covered and how a claim works. A long warranty with a difficult claim process is worth less than a shorter one that is simple to use.

What if my battery fails because of the alternator?

A fault in the car's charging system is usually not covered by the battery's warranty. A test will show where the fault is.
