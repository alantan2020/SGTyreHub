What Should a Quoted Tyre Price Include in Singapore?

Two quotes for the same tyre can differ by a lot, and the cheaper-looking one is not always cheaper once the extras are added. Here is what to check so you compare like with like.

What Should a Quoted Tyre Price Include in Singapore?

Start with the exact tyre, not just the size

A quote should name the brand, the model line and the full size, for example the load index and speed rating as well as the width, profile and rim size. Two tyres in the same size can be very different products. Also ask for the production week (the DOT code) if you care about freshness, because stock can sit for a long time. Our guide on reading the DOT code explains how to check it.

Is fitting included?

Some quotes show the tyre alone and add fitting later. Ask whether the price covers removing the old tyre, mounting the new tyre and refitting the wheel on your car. A quote that says 'per tyre' and a quote that says 'for four tyres, fitted' are not the same thing.

Is wheel balancing included?

Balancing stops vibration at speed and uneven wear. It is normally done every time a new tyre is mounted, so it should be part of a fitted quote. If balancing is listed as an extra, add it before you compare. Wheel alignment is different: it is a separate service and is usually quoted separately. Our guide on rotation, balancing and alignment explains which one fixes which problem.

The small items that are easy to miss

Ask about new valves, because rubber valves age and many shops replace them with new tyres. Ask whether disposal of the old tyres is included, and whether there is any extra charge for larger rims, low-profile or run-flat tyres, which take more time to fit. Ask whether the price already includes GST, because a quote before GST and a quote after GST are not comparable.

Warranty: what is covered and what is not

A fair quote tells you what happens if something goes wrong. Manufacturing defects in the tyre are normally covered by the maker. Problems that come from fitting, such as a leaking valve or a balancing fault, should be covered by whoever fitted the tyre, and usually show up within days. Damage from the road, such as a nail, a cut or a pothole, is normally not covered. Ask for the terms in writing, and be careful with any promise that sounds too broad.

A simple way to compare

Ask each supplier the same questions: exact tyre and production week, fitted or not, balancing included, valves and disposal, GST, and warranty terms. Then compare the total you would actually pay on the day. Common questions

Is wheel alignment included in a tyre price?

Usually not. Alignment is a separate service, quoted separately. Balancing, on the other hand, is normally done with every new tyre.

Why do two quotes for the same size differ so much?

The tyres may be different brands or model lines, one quote may exclude fitting, balancing or GST, or the tyres may have different production dates. Compare the full list, not only the headline number.

Should I ask for the tyre's production date?

Yes. The DOT code on the sidewall shows the week and year of manufacture. Stock that has been stored for a long time is still usable within reason, but you should know what you are buying.

Are run-flat or low-profile tyres more expensive to fit?

They can be, because they take more time and care to mount. Ask whether the quote already allows for this.
