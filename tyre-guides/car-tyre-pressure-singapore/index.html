Car Tyre Pressure in Singapore: What to Use and When to Check

Correct tyre pressure is a simple, low-cost way to make tyres last longer and keep the car safe. It is also the check most people skip.

Car Tyre Pressure in Singapore: What to Use and When to Check

Where to find the right pressure

The correct pressure for your car is on a sticker, usually inside the driver's door frame or in the fuel flap. It is also in the owner's manual. It often shows different pressures for the front and the rear, and for a normal load or a full load.

Do not use the number on the tyre

The pressure printed on the tyre sidewall is the maximum the tyre can take. It is not the pressure to use. Always follow the car maker's figure on the sticker.

Check when the tyres are cold

Pressure rises as tyres heat up from driving or from sun on a hot day. For a reliable reading, check before you drive, or after the car has been parked for a few hours. If you have to check after driving, do not let air out to bring it down to the sticker figure.

What goes wrong

Low pressure makes the tyre flex more, which builds heat, wears the outer edges and uses more fuel. Too high pressure wears the centre of the tread and reduces the grip area. Either way, the tyre does not do its job as well.

How often

Check once a month, and before a long drive, with the spare as well if you have one. If a tyre keeps losing air, there may be a slow puncture or a leaking valve, and it is worth having it looked at.

Common questions

My car has a tyre pressure warning light. Does that replace manual checks?

No. The system warns you when pressure is already low by a set amount. A regular manual check catches it earlier.

Should I use a higher pressure for a full load?

Many cars list a higher pressure for a full load on the same sticker. Follow it when the car is loaded.

Is nitrogen better than air?

Nitrogen holds pressure slightly longer, but the right pressure and regular checking matter far more than the gas used.
